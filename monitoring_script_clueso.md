# PEN-Plus NCD Monitoring App — Video Script

[on screen: Voiceover script for a 7 minute 31 second tablet screen recording, with an Intro and an Outro slide. Each section heading gives the recording's start and end time.]

## Intro slide

[on screen: Title "PEN-Plus NCD Monitoring App". Subtitle "A walkthrough for mentors".]

PEN-Plus NCD Monitoring. A short walkthrough for mentors.

## 0:00–0:14 — Welcome and selecting your identity

[on screen: Tap the initials in the top-right corner to open the setup screen. Select the demo user.]

Welcome to the PEN-Plus NCD monitoring app. Mentors use it to record and follow up on mentees' clinical competencies. First, the mentor selects their name from the evaluator list, so sessions are credited to them.

## 0:14–0:44 — Home screen and navigation

[on screen: The New Session and My Mentees buttons, the recent sessions list, and the sync status line. Then point to the header.]

This is the home screen. Both main buttons open the mentee list, because every session starts with a mentee. Recent sessions are listed below, and the status line shows whether the device is syncing.

The app is designed for tablets, but works on any device, and it keeps working without a connection. On every screen, Back at the top left returns you to the previous page.

## 0:44–1:16 — The mentee list

[on screen: Tap My Mentees.]

This is the mentee list. You can search by name or facility, or add a new mentee. Each card shows the mentee's profession, district and facility, how many sessions they've had, and any gaps logged.

Let's find Demo Mentee 051.

## 1:16–2:00 — The mentee profile

[on screen: Search for Demo Mentee 051 and open their profile.]

This is the mentee's profile. It lists their evaluation journeys. A journey is one mentee's progress on one clinical tool over time, and each mentorship visit is recorded as a session within it.

Each journey shows the number of sessions, the last visit, the phase or competency status, and any open gaps. Journeys are kept separate by tool, so a mentee can work on diabetes and respiratory care at the same time, each with its own history.

## 2:00–2:05 — Choosing a clinical tool

[on screen: Tap Start New Session and select Echocardiogram.]

I'll start a new session and choose Echocardiogram, the shortest tool.

## 2:05–2:42 — Planning the assessment

[on screen: The planning step. Point out the date field, the item sections, Select all and Clear, the counselling section, and the grey advanced item. Select a few items. Tap Confirm plan.]

First, the mentor plans what to observe today. Items are grouped into the same sections as the paper tool.

You don't have to observe every item at every visit. Just select what's relevant. The general counselling competencies are included in every session, whatever the tool.

Items shaded grey are advanced competencies. They aren't required for a mentee to be considered competent, but they can still be assessed. I'll confirm the plan.

## 2:42–4:14 — Recording observations and scores

[on screen: The observation list. Score all planned items. Show the meaning chip under an item, and the Improved indicator. Mark one counselling item N/A.]

Here is the observation list for today's plan. Each item has six buttons: scores 1 to 5, and N-A.

When I tap a score, its meaning appears underneath, in the same colour as the legend. Here, 4 means the mentee demonstrates advanced competency. N-A means the item couldn't be evaluated during this visit.

The app records the mentee's score only. There's no separate mentor or autonomy score.

Because this mentee has been assessed before, each item shows the previous score and its date. After I score, the app shows whether the mentee has improved, regressed, or stayed the same.

Each item also has an optional note, for what was observed, or why an item couldn't be evaluated.

A visit can also include more than one observation round, for example the ward in the morning and the outpatient clinic in the afternoon. Rounds belong to the same visit, and I can switch between them at any time.

## 4:14–4:39 — Recording Echocardiogram activity

[on screen: Scroll to the action bar. Review session is disabled, and the bar asks for the echocardiogram counts. On the Echocardiogram activity card, enter 2 performed under supervision and 1 with a correct final diagnosis. Fill in the session notes, then tap Review session.]

Review session is still disabled, and the bar tells me why: the echocardiogram counts are missing.

They're entered once for the whole visit. Today, the mentee performed two supervised echocardiograms, and one reached the correct final diagnosis. I'll add a session note, and the session is ready to review.

## 4:39–5:21 — Reviewing and saving the session

[on screen: Tap Review session. Show the outcome card at the top first. Then show the summary row, an average tile, the item chips, and a change marker. Tap Save Session in the bottom bar.]

The review starts with the outcome. Here it says Not yet competent, because some items score below 4, and it lists them. Once every required item reaches 4 or 5, it says the journey will reach Basic Competent instead.

Averages show their meaning: here, the tool average is in the Basic band. Each item shows its score, what it means, and any change since the last session.

If something needs fixing, Back to Edit keeps everything in place. Otherwise, I save. It's stored on the tablet straight away, and syncs when online.

## 5:21–5:54 — Journey history

[on screen: After saving, the app returns to the mentee profile, where the Echocardiogram journey is now at the top showing Ongoing. Open the journey. Show the summary card, the session list and the latest round.]

After saving, the app returns to the profile. The Echocardiogram journey is now at the top, in the Ongoing phase.

Inside the journey, the summary shows the number of sessions, the latest average and date. The session list shows every visit in date order, with its round for that day. I can open any session to see its full scores, and Edit corrects a mistake if needed.

## 5:54–6:50 — Competencies grouped like the paper tool

[on screen: Tap Start New Session and choose Diabetes Mellitus. In the plan, scroll down to show how the items are grouped as in the tool document. Show selecting all items in a group, clearing them, and selecting a few. Tap Back to leave the plan, then confirm Discard in the prompt.]

Next, a quick look at how closely the app follows the PEN-Plus paper tools.

Diabetes Mellitus has 37 competencies. They're grouped into the same sections as the paper form. Within each section, the mentor can select every item, clear them, or pick just the ones planned for this visit. That keeps each session focused, and makes it easy to cover the whole tool over several visits.

We've already been through scoring, so I'll leave this plan. The app checks before discarding unsaved work, and I'll confirm.

## 6:50–7:31 — Sync and wrap-up

[on screen: Tap the sync button in the header to open Sync Data. Point out Sync All. Don't run Storage Cleanup. Return home.]

The app syncs automatically whenever it's online. Work is saved on the tablet first, so nothing is lost offline. This sync page is a fallback. It shows when data last synced, and Sync All pulls the latest from the server. Before leaving a facility, check here that your sessions have synced.

That's the workflow: choose a mentee and a tool, plan, observe, review, save, and follow progress over time. Thank you for watching. We welcome your feedback.
